A Race to the Top: The Adoption of Net‐Zero City Pledges in Large US Cities

ABSTRACT Many cities have adopted net‐zero emission pledges, yet little is known about why these commitments emerge or how their determinants shift under changing policy environments. Using a panel of 148 large US cities from 2016 to 2022, this study applies the Political Market Framework and employs discrete‐time event‐history models to examine how policy demand, policy supply and institutional conditions shape adoption decisions. Results show that civic capacity, political ideology, and horizontal diffusion drive adoption in the full period, but their influence varies sharply across institutional contexts. During federal disengagement (2016–2018), cities with less population, strong vulnerability signals, supportive state policies, or fiscal cushioning through federal aid are more likely to adopt. Improving Cooperation in Government Purchasing Through Formal‐Relational Contracting

ABSTRACT A central challenge in government contracting is fostering cooperation between buyers and sellers where contract terms are incomplete. This article introduces a framework for formal‐relational contracting (FRC) in public sector contexts, where rigid regulations, competing values, and market imperfections make cooperation both difficult and valuable. FRC promotes cooperation through contract terms defining shared goals, guiding principles, and governance mechanisms. We present the theoretical foundations of FRC and examine its potential through 320 government contract management decision simulations where public contract managers acted as government buyers and business sellers facing sequential choices under both FRC contracts and the more conventional formal contracting approach.
