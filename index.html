Reply to Kettl: The Field of Public Administration in America and an Unpreparedness of Its Own Making?

ABSTRACT This Reply suggests the sub‐field of U.S.‐specific public administration is under‐prepared to address democratic deconsolidation given its multi‐decade distance from the disciplines of comparative politics and political economy and from the sub‐fields of comparative and development public administration. The narrowness of what is understand as “good” public administration by U.S.‐based scholars has largely excluded cross‐disciplinary and non‐U.S. intellectual histories which, if understood, would have deepened sub‐field intellectual depth and breadth but also, better prepared the sub‐field for America's current administrative state. This Reply suggests the value of drawing upon post‐WWII administration‐linked trends in other disciplines and upon links between administration and prior democratic deconsolidations (the U.S. is not unique).
