ABSTRACT Switzerland presents a most peculiar political system whose institutional idiosyncrasies shape not only the practice of public administration but also the scientific study thereof. This country study presents the development and state of both public administration as practice and Public Administration as a scholarly discipline. It argues that both are largely determined by three properties of Switzerland as a political context and field of study: Semi‐direct democracy turns the administration into an important political actor.
