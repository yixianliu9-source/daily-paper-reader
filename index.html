ABSTRACT The relationship between inequity and administrative burden constitutes a pivotal concern in the realm of public administration and policy, but how policy inequity (i.e., inequity in policy implementation) influences administrative burden has garnered scant attention. The present study employs data about public assistance programs in China spanning from 2015 to 2017 to examine the consequences of policy inequity on administrative burden. The empirical findings underscore that policy inequity augments psychological and compliance costs while concurrently diminishing citizens' participation in welfare programs, thereby indicating that policy inequity intensifies administrative burden. ABSTRACT Democratic backsliding has emerged as a defining political challenge of the contemporary era, yet systematic cross‐national evidence linking it to governance outcomes remains scarce. This article argues that backsliding affects governance through two analytically distinct channels: a political accountability channel, whereby weakened contestation and oversight reduce the external costs of poor performance; and an administrative channel, through which executive politicization erodes meritocratic recruitment and bureaucratic impartiality.
