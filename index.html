Restoring the Deformed Administrative State? Bureaucratic Repair After Illiberal Rule

ABSTRACT While scholarship has documented how populist and authoritarian‐leaning executives deform bureaucratic institutions, post‐illiberal reconstruction has only begun to receive attention. This paper develops an exploratory framework distinguishing four repair strategies (neutralizing, rectifying, balancing, and democratic hardening) and two repair modes (formal‐institutional and normative‐capacity repair). Applying the heuristic to Poland, Brazil, and Hungary, we posit that the selection and limits of repair strategies follow the gap between the depth of illiberal entrenchment and the legal‐political capacity of successor governments to overcome veto players and enact rectifying reforms. Within the periods observed, formal‐institutional repair has outpaced the reconstruction of professional norms, expertise, and trust, and repair has produced layered administrative orders in which illiberal‐era instruments partly persist and thus remain available to future governments. Investor Orientation and Commitment in Outcomes‐Based Public Service Contracts

ABSTRACT Outcomes‐based contracts (OBCs) organize public service delivery around measurable results, yet little is known about how different investors engage with these instruments. This article examines how investor orientation (social, financial, or dual‐purpose) shapes investor commitment in OBCs worldwide. Drawing on a global dataset of 338 contracts, we combine descriptive analyses, regression models, and qualitative interviews to investigate how investor orientation relates to investment scale. We hypothesize that OBCs involving socially oriented investors are associated with higher levels of investment than those involving financially or dual‐purpose investors. Consistent with this prediction, socially oriented investors consistently commit larger amounts, whereas financially oriented and dual‐purpose investors typically participate with smaller contributions.
