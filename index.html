Steering and Setting Free: Insights From a 20‐Year Retrospective Case Study of a Security and Justice Organization's Digital Transformation

ABSTRACT Advances in digital technologies are expected to fundamentally change how organizations operate and create value. Empirical studies show that public organizations face difficulties organizing and sustaining this digital transformation. To better understand these challenges, this study examines the organizational mechanisms underlying digital transformation in a public organization over the past 20 years. Our findings show that this transformation was primarily enabled by an emergent change approach, with influential planned changes occurring only occasionally. We identify the organizational mechanisms underlying both types of change and analyze their interdependence. Between the Lines: Decoding Signaling Differences in Public and Private Sector Job Advertisements

ABSTRACT To compete in the “war for talent,” public sector employers must demonstrate advantages over private employers. While public service motivation (PSM) predicts sector preference, scholars disagree on whether public employers (can) effectively signal PSM in job advertisements. Consequently, this article examines how public and private job advertisements differ in language. We collected 5000 advertisements from both sectors and performed natural language processing (NLP) and qualitative content analysis. Both sectors use specialized language that can narrow the applicant pool. PSM signals appear more often in the public sector, but affective motives are rarely communicated; instead, self‐sacrifice is framed as a legal obligation.
