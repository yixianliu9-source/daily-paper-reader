The Practice and Origins of Performance Measurement: Assessing the Sunnyvale Model, 1984–2023

ABSTRACT Few regulatory reforms are as celebrated or emulated as the performance measurement system of Sunnyvale, California, which inspired the Government Performance and Results Act of 1993 and countless other national and local efforts. This study presents the first longitudinal, cross‐programmatic analysis of a performance measurement system, introducing a digital archival approach to analyze 152 performance indicators across 35,000 pages of documents from 1984 to 2023, supplemented with interviews of key stakeholders. Even if Sunnyvale's system initially functioned as an exemplar of performance‐based decision‐making, it deteriorated with weakened commitment and, more importantly, sustained significant implementation costs while its benefits reduced over time. Why Politicians Misbehave: A Systematic Review of How Attributes of Political Office Affect Moral Decision‐Making and Behavior

ABSTRACT The moral decision‐making and behavior of politicians is influenced by the distinctive and characteristic attributes of political office. This literature review analyzes 80 articles from public administration, political science and related disciplines to systematically map these attributes and their underlying mechanisms. This study identifies six categories of attributes of political office that relate to moral decision‐making and behavior: political system design, political role, relations to societal actors, legal and regulatory measures, political cycle, and some general attributes such as the size of the political institution.
